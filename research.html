Other teams’ ideas and how ours compares

Public strategy research checked October 5, 2026.

Working draft · October 5, 2026 · Agreed direction plus clearly marked proposals

What the sources can and cannot establish

I found a team-authored strategy guide and early-season discussions proposing several recognizable approaches. These show ideas people are considering; they do not establish which plan wins reliably in current tournaments. Targeted searches for later match analysis did not produce a sufficiently documented match set to support a win-rate claim. No tournament video was scored for this review.

Concentrate yellow value and defend

VEX Forum: Override strat, May–June 2026 includes proposals to build tall yellow stacks, focus on one quadrant, and defend its toggle. Our plan shares the concentration idea, but adds an explicit stable-color base, productive alternate routes and a conditional move to Down. The thread also contains uncertainty and corrections; it is discussion, not authoritative rules guidance.

Capture, steal, defend

Spartan Design: Override Toggle Strategy separates early capture, late stealing and guarding valuable toggles. It emphasizes the opportunity cost of a defender and the large value swing from yellow ownership. Our plan is more specific about robot roles and recovery routes. Their late-steal framing exposes a hole in our initial reasoning: blue need not defend a stolen toggle for the entire match if it can affect the final state.

MID as an alternative investment

The forum discussion also considers investing in MID. The useful strategic question is whether a majority finish is easier to secure than a perimeter toggle. Some early suggestions rely on putting a yellow on MID during endgame; the current placement restriction makes that timing unsuitable. Plan the investment before the cutoff, then evaluate the majority contest separately. Current SG12.

Cycle efficiency is useful, but final ownership matters

Spartan’s scoring and strategy framework recommends comparing complete cycle times and fitting roles to the partner. We can use those questions, but should not import its score examples: the page incorrectly gives unowned yellows a base value and describes an auto tie as no bonus. Our calculator instead uses visible halves, zero yellow value without ownership, and six auto points per alliance for a tie.

Our own adjustment: compare points retained at the finish, not just points temporarily added per second. Also, two capable scorers are not automatically a poor pairing. Our separate-goal plan can keep both productive if routes and supplies allow it. Neither assumption is settled by a generic robot label.

Prestacks, loaders and early toggle behavior

VEX Forum: strategy discussion, April–June 2026 discusses taking prestacks before moving toward loaders and reports early examples of teams flipping and leaving. That resembles our cleanup opening and opportunistic toggle recovery. It also discusses consistency versus risky plays. These are dated participant observations and proposals, not a match dataset I independently verified.
