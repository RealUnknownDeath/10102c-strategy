10102C / Override

1. Definitions

A shared vocabulary for reading the field and explaining the next decision.

Reference · October 6, 2026 · Agreed direction plus clearly marked proposals

Read these terms first, then the maneuvers. Field and scoring entries summarize the existing scoring reference; strategic terms describe our team’s working language, not additional official rules.

Field labels

Our diagrams use Left, Down, Top and Right for the four toggle areas. Left contains R1/N2; Down R2/N4; Top N1/B1; Right B2/N3. These goal names are our team labels. North is up on the board; 10102C normally starts at the bottom.

Alliance and neutral goals

R1/R2 are our red alliance goals. N1–N4 are neutral goals. Being close to us does not make a neutral goal ours to clear. The own-goal removal fallback does not apply to N2 or N4.

Pins, cups and visible halves

A pin has colored halves; cups and stack arrangement affect which halves remain visible. Count exposed halves rather than whole pins. Visible red/blue halves score five for their alliance; yellow halves score ten for the owner of their area. “Score reds” means improve our net visible-color result, not merely carry a pin containing red.

Stacks and upright pickups

A stack is the assembled scoring material on a goal or available as a pickup. Upright cup-pin stacks can be quick claw pickups. Extra height, access and cup orientation can change the next cycle; a standing stack is not automatically the best pickup.

Loaders

The field supply locations marked L on our boards. Upper and lower refer to our top-down map. Access to a loader and a usable scoring route are separate requirements.

Toggle ownership and neutralization

A perimeter toggle determines its area’s yellow ownership. Neutralization removes that yellow value from either alliance; capture by blue awards it to blue. Final contact can neutralize a defended toggle. Full blue capture is a separate defensive-failure branch, not our baseline when zone defense succeeds.

Zone control and coverage

Our team terms for controlling the approach and keeping a reliable response available. Zone control can return before we flip the toggle. Coverage means a robot can actually prevent or answer the threat; standing nearby or having a red toggle alone does not prove coverage.

Scoring cycle and local scoring

A cycle includes collecting, travelling, placing and becoming ready for the next useful action. Local scoring stays within a route that preserves the assigned toggle coverage. A nearby goal is not automatically a safe cycle.

Return window

The time and clear route available to return or intercept before an opponent can exploit the area we leave. This depends on both opponents, facing and obstacles. We have not measured a universal distance or time threshold.

Approach, threat and feint

An approach moves an opponent toward a relevant lane or toggle. A credible threat can beat our response if we continue the current action. A feint aims to interrupt us without committing. We still need to define where an approach becomes a stop-scoring threat.

Scoring matchup

Whether our pair can outscore their pair on the routes actually available. This is independent of who won auto. Start with winning/losing; refine later by individual scoring and defensive ability.

Auto result

Win, loss or tie is an input to driver control. The bonus and auto-scored objects are counted separately. Routine design is deferred; inspect actual stacks and access rather than assuming a perfect finish.

Safe margin of victory

A projected lead that exceeds the credible remaining adverse swing and uncertainty for the chosen finish. A current lead alone is insufficient. Unknown remaining danger is not zero. If leaving risks our defended zone or margin, stay and score locally only while coverage remains intact.

Projected finish and remaining danger

The selected final ownership, visible halves, auto result and parked robots form the projected finish. Remaining danger is additional adverse change beyond that state. Do not subtract the same lost toggle, park or MID points twice.

Parking and MID

Each robot ending in MID adds eight points; robot majority determines MID yellow ownership and a tie gives neither alliance those yellows. Object placement on MID must finish before the final ten seconds. A parking trip still needs a safe departure and return assessment.
