10102C / Override

Trying to beat our own plan

Analytical counterplay, not simulated match results.

Working draft · October 6, 2026 · Agreed direction plus clearly marked proposals

These are adversarial thought experiments. No win rate, measured driving time or physical defense success is claimed. Each branch gives blue a purposeful response instead of assuming it follows our preferred route.

Mirror match: both alliances use our plan

If both sides score reds, build local yellow stacks and prepare two defenders, neither gains an advantage merely by “making them camp.” Both pay that cost. With equal visible color and yellow value, auto and the eventual MID/ownership finish decide the difference. Losing auto means an otherwise symmetric finish still loses; we need an actual extra scoring or control gain.

The most important assumptions to challenge

We have assumed a return route stays available, the weaker robot can hold Down, useful yellows and cups remain, and the stronger scorer can work south while covering Left. All four can fail together late in a match. The plan needs a fallback for losing one area, not merely instructions to defend both harder.

New proposal · needs practice

Give up the lower-value area deliberately when simultaneous defense is impossible, but only after comparing the full finish. Keep the stronger position and continue scoring if that beats a failed attempt to save everything.

A repeatable practice sequence

Start from the same count and positions. Run blue as a pure scorer, then a weak camper plus strong scorer, then a late split attack, then a mirror of our plan. Repeat with auto win/tie/loss. Record final margin, why the chosen finish failed, and which assumption changed. Do not average away the specific losing mechanism.
