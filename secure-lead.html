10102C / Override

When is the lead actually safe?

First remove the points we might lose. Then allow for what they can still gain.

Working draft · October 5, 2026 · Agreed direction plus clearly marked proposals

Which finish this conservative test describes

This is the stay-and-defend branch. We keep control of the zone, but final contact—from being pushed into the toggle or an opponent reaching it—may neutralize the yellows. We do not assume blue captures the toggle while that defense succeeds. Other safe finishes, including parking, taking an open toggle, or continued local scoring, use their own final-state inputs.

The idea in plain language

Start with our red-half points minus their blue-half points. Add or subtract auto. Then evaluate the yellow ownership and parking finish we are willing to accept. Finally subtract an allowance for extra damage they can still do before time runs out. If the remainder is positive, that finish survives the chosen allowance. If the allowance is unknown, the calculation cannot certify the lead.

Stable starting marginColor points + auto result
Chosen finishYellow ownership + robot counts in MID
Remaining dangerExtra scoring, ownership or visibility changes, and counting uncertainty

Your no-park, yellows-neutralized test

Assume our perimeter yellows go neutral, neither red robot parks, both blue robots park, and blue owns MID yellows. Keep any other blue-owned yellows in the count too.

Finish margin = color-point lead + auto margin − 16 − blue-owned yellow points.
Here blue-owned yellow points include MID and any other blue-owned quadrants. Our neutralized yellows contribute zero. A full blue capture is a separate defensive-failure scenario, not the baseline assumption while we successfully defend the zone.

The more general version is: 5 × (red halves − blue halves) + auto margin + 10 × (red-owned yellow halves − blue-owned yellow halves) + 8 × (red parks − blue parks). Auto margin is +12, 0 or −12. Count auto-scored objects in the field once, then add the bonus once.

Why the example is fragile

The preset has 90 red-color points against 40 blue-color points: a 50-point base lead. An auto win adds 12. Giving blue 16 parking points and 40 yellow points leaves only 6 points. One additional blue-owned visible yellow half is enough to overturn that finish. An auto tie makes the same field a 6-point loss; an auto loss makes it an 18-point loss.

How many more yellows do we need?

Take the projected deficit plus the remaining-danger allowance. Add enough reliably retained visible yellow halves to exceed that amount, at ten points each. A tie is not enough. If our finish margin is −18 and the extra-danger allowance is 10, three retained yellow halves add 30 and leave a two-point cushion. Those halves do not help if we are still assuming their toggle goes neutral.

Our color lead is a base, not an absolute guarantee

Color ownership does not flip with a toggle. However, an exposed red half on a neutral goal can lose visibility if a later cup legally covers it. An own-goal removal can also sacrifice red halves. Include those possible losses in the extra-danger allowance; a lead in colored halves alone does not prove the finish is safe.

Do not double-count danger

This calculator already scores the selected final ownership and parks. The extra allowance is only additional adverse margin change beyond that state. Do not subtract the same MID loss, park or toggle flip twice. If a yellow moves from our ownership to theirs, the margin changes by twenty per half; moving to neutral changes it by ten.

Build believable opponent branches

Evaluate a two-park finish, a one-park/one-toggle attack, and a two-toggle attack separately. Simultaneously granting blue two parks and two distant toggle contacts is a conservative arithmetic envelope, not necessarily an attainable two-robot finish. Earlier theft followed by parking can be attainable if we cannot recover it. The route and remaining time decide which branch matters.

New proposal · needs practice

Once measured, use the opponents’ fastest credible remaining cycles and attack routes to bound extra danger. Until then, enter a deliberately cautious allowance and call the result conditional. Do not replace missing timing data with zero.
