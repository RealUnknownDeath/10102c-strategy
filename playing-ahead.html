Playing with a lead

Keep scoring until the lead survives their remaining options.

Working draft · October 6, 2026 · Agreed direction plus clearly marked proposals

Protect a safe margin of victory

Agreed October 6: We always aim for a safe winning margin. If leaving creates any risk to the toggle zone we are defending or to that margin, we stay in the zone. We can keep scoring locally until the end, provided the scoring action preserves coverage.

Camping both toggles is a conditional finish, not our default. When departure is safe, we may park or take an open toggle with useful yellow value. Leave only when they cannot take the area we vacate before time expires, or we can reliably return or intercept them in time. If that is uncertain, stay.

Parking generally preserves a shorter return route in our intended setup. A last-second trip to another perimeter toggle may not. Check the actual positions and route before either move; extra points never justify risking the defended zone.

A visible lead is not yet a secure lead

In the defended-zone finish, our baseline worst case is contact neutralizing our yellows. A full capture belongs to a separate defensive-failure branch. Before committing to this finish, evaluate the likely finish after giving blue the MID and parking outcome we are prepared to concede. Then allow for what they can still score or steal. What we should watch

Watch Blue 2, not only the robot at our toggle. A defender can feint to draw 10102C away while the free scorer takes the other approach. Count the points tied to each quadrant and compare that exposure with our non-yellow margin.

New proposal · needs practice

Maintain two spoken estimates: “ahead if our yellows hold” and “ahead if our yellows disappear.” A team can be winning the first comparison while losing the second. That distinction tells us how much the finish depends on our yellows; it does not authorize a risky departure from the defended zone.

A positive calculator margin is conditional on its finish state and future-swing allowance. It does not prove that our robots can enforce those assumptions.
