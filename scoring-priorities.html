Scoring priorities and goal selection

Build stable color points and place yellows where we can still control their value.

Working draft · October 5, 2026 · Agreed direction plus clearly marked proposals

Agreed direction

Our default yellow goals are R1 and N2. Red halves usually go to N1 and R2, with N4 and MID as additional options. This preserves convenient yellow space near Left. R2 can receive yellows when needed; the Down-control branch also deliberately adds yellow value to R2 and N4.

What counts as a useful red cycle?

Choose the visible result, not just the pin’s name. If both halves of a red/yellow pin remain visible on N1 while Top belongs to blue, it gives us 5 and blue 10: our margin gets five points worse. A red/blue pin with both halves visible gives five to each side and does not improve the margin. Cup orientation and the next addition to the stack can change that result. Count the entire visible mix before calling a cycle “red scoring.”

The important correction

R1 and N2 are both close to Left, but only R1 is our alliance goal. We cannot knock down N2 to erase its yellow liability. “Near us” and “removable by us” are different properties.
New proposal · needs practice

Before adding another yellow half, ask: which final toggle outcome makes this worth points, and who can preserve that outcome? If the answer is unclear, a slower but reliable red cycle may be more useful.

Color points can still change

Red halves do not depend on a toggle, but that does not make every red half permanent. On a shared neutral goal, a later legal cup placement can hide an exposed half. Our red alliance goals have different protection from opponent interaction. Preserve useful red scoring on R2 when practical, and include possible visibility changes in the finish estimate. SC3 and SG9–SG10.

Avoid a false “full” rule

There is no measured practical stack limit for our bot yet. “Full” means the next addition is too slow, unreliable or out of reach for this robot—not an invented universal pin count. Cup supply, orientation and visibility also affect the real return.
